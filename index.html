A little about these recipes

Cameroonian and Ghanaian cooking are wonderfully varied. These approachable, home-style recipes celebrate bold leaves, groundnuts, palm oil, plantains, rice, and the joy of eating together. Adjust the pepper and seasonings to your taste.

🥬01 · Littoral

Bitterleaf · groundnut · shrimp

Ndolé

Serves 6

A rich, comforting stew where gently bitter ndolé leaves meet creamy groundnuts and savory seafood.

⏱ About 1 hr 30 min🍳 Simmer

Ingredients

  • 500 g washed ndolé (bitterleaf), fresh or thawed
  • 500 g beef chuck, cut into bite-size pieces
  • 250 g raw shelled peanuts, or unsweetened groundnut paste
  • 200 g peeled shrimp
  • 2 tbsp dried crayfish or dried shrimp, ground
  • 2 onions, 3 garlic cloves, and a thumb of ginger
  • 3 tbsp vegetable oil; salt and pepper
  • Fresh chilli or seasoning cube, optional

Method

  1. If using fresh ndolé, rinse and blanch it briefly. Drain and rinse again, pressing out excess water. Keep a little bitterness for the dish's characteristic flavor.
  2. Put the beef in a pot with half a chopped onion, salt, and enough water to cover. Simmer until tender, about 45–60 minutes; save the broth.
  3. Boil the peanuts for 10 minutes, drain, then blend with a little water into a smooth paste. (Skip this step if using groundnut paste.)
  4. Warm the oil in a pot. Soften the remaining onion, garlic, and ginger. Stir in the peanut paste and 2 cups of broth; simmer gently for 20 minutes, stirring often.
  5. Add beef, ndolé, and ground crayfish. Simmer for 15 minutes. Fry the shrimp separately until just pink, then fold them in and season to taste. Serve with boiled plantain, rice, or miondo.
🌿02 · Southwest

Eru leaves · waterleaf · palm oil

Eru

Serves 6

Finely shredded eru cooks down with tender greens, smoked fish, and palm oil. Traditionally enjoyed with water fufu or garri.

⏱ About 1 hr 30 min🍳 One-pot

Ingredients

  • 300 g finely shredded eru leaves
  • 500 g waterleaf, chopped (or spinach as an easy substitute)
  • 400 g stewing beef, cubed
  • 250 g cow skin, cleaned and cut up, optional
  • 150 g smoked fish, deboned
  • 3 tbsp ground crayfish
  • 150 ml red palm oil
  • 1 onion, salt, and chilli to taste

Method

  1. Place the beef and cow skin in a pot with the chopped onion, salt, and enough water to cover. Simmer until tender, about 45–60 minutes.
  2. Add the smoked fish and cook for 5 minutes. Keep some of the cooking liquid in the pot; it will help the greens soften.
  3. Add the waterleaf, cover, and cook for 8–10 minutes until wilted. Stir in the shredded eru and cook uncovered for 10–15 minutes, stirring occasionally.
  4. Add crayfish, palm oil, and chilli. Simmer for another 5–10 minutes, until the greens are tender and the mixture is moist but not soupy. Taste and adjust salt. Serve hot with garri or water fufu.
🫘03 · West & Littoral

Black-eyed peas · palm oil · banana leaf

Koki

Serves 6

A savory, steamed bean pudding wrapped in leaves. The orange-red color comes from palm oil whisked into the bean batter.

⏱ 30 min prep + soaking · 1 hr 30 min cook🍳 Steam

Ingredients

  • 500 g dried black-eyed peas
  • 180 ml red palm oil, warmed until pourable
  • 1–2 hot chillies, finely chopped
  • About 150 ml warm water, as needed for blending
  • Salt to taste
  • Banana leaves, softened over a flame or in hot water
  • Kitchen string; spinach or tender cocoyam leaves, optional

Method

  1. Soak the beans overnight, or for at least 6 hours. Rub handfuls between your palms under water to loosen the skins, then rinse until most skins float away.
  2. Drain and blend the peeled beans with just enough warm water to make a thick, smooth paste. Transfer to a bowl and beat for a minute to lighten it.
  3. Whisk in the warm palm oil gradually. Stir in chilli and salt. The batter should be thick and orange; add water a spoonful at a time only if it is too stiff.
  4. Spoon the batter into softened banana leaves, fold into tightly closed parcels, and tie with string. Line a pot with spare leaves and add water beneath a steaming rack or trivet.
  5. Arrange parcels above the water, cover, and steam gently for 1½ hours, checking that the pot does not run dry. Let cool slightly before unwrapping. Serve with boiled plantain or cassava.
🍠04 · Northwest

Pounded cocoyam · yellow soup

Achu & yellow soup

Serves 6

Soft, stretchy cocoyam fufu served with a fragrant yellow palm-oil soup and tender meat. A celebratory meal from the Grassfields.

⏱ About 1 hr 30 min🍳 Boil & blend

Ingredients

  • 1.5 kg cocoyam (taro), washed
  • 700 g assorted beef and cow skin, cut up
  • 700 ml reserved meat stock
  • 120 ml red palm oil
  • 1 tbsp achu spice blend
  • A small amount of food-grade potash (kanwa), dissolved in warm water and settled
  • Salt and chilli to taste

Important: Use only food-grade potash sold for cooking. Do not use building lime or unknown mineral products.

Method

  1. Boil the meat and cow skin in salted water until tender, about 45–60 minutes. Keep about 700 ml of the broth warm.
  2. In a separate pot, boil the cocoyam with its skin until a knife slides in easily. Drain, peel while warm, and pound until smooth and stretchy. A sturdy food processor also works; add very little warm water if needed. Keep covered and warm.
  3. Warm the palm oil gently; do not let it smoke. Dissolve a small pinch of food-grade potash in a few tablespoons of warm water and let the solids settle. Use only the clear liquid.
  4. Blend the warm stock with the achu spice and palm oil. Add the clear potash liquid a little at a time while blending until the soup turns yellow and lightly thickens. Season to taste.
  5. Serve the soup alongside the pounded cocoyam and meat. Adjust the soup with more warm stock if it becomes too thick.
🍗05 · A favorite everywhere

Chicken · ripe plantain · vegetables

Poulet DG

Serves 6

Golden chicken and sweet fried plantain tossed with vegetables in a ginger-scented tomato sauce. DG stands for Directeur Général.

⏱ About 1 hr 15 min🍳 Pan-fry & simmer

Ingredients

  • 1.5 kg chicken pieces
  • 4 ripe but firm plantains, peeled and sliced
  • 3 carrots, sliced; 200 g green beans, trimmed
  • 1 red and 1 green bell pepper, sliced
  • 4 tomatoes, chopped; 2 onions, sliced
  • 4 garlic cloves and a thumb of fresh ginger, grated
  • 3 tbsp vegetable oil, plus oil for frying plantain
  • Parsley or celery, salt, pepper, and chilli to taste

Method

  1. Season the chicken with garlic, ginger, salt, and pepper. Brown in a little hot oil in batches; set aside. If using firm chicken, simmer it with a splash of water until nearly tender before continuing.
  2. Fry the plantain slices until golden on both sides. Drain on a plate lined with paper towel.
  3. In a large pan, soften the onions in the remaining oil. Add tomatoes, peppers, parsley or celery, and chilli. Cook for 8–10 minutes until the tomatoes form a thick sauce.
  4. Add the chicken and a small splash of water or stock. Cover and simmer until cooked through and tender. Stir in carrots and green beans; cook until just tender.
  5. Fold in the fried plantain gently and warm for 2–3 minutes so it keeps its shape. Taste, adjust seasoning, and serve hot.
🍚06 · Ghana

Rice · tomato · pepper

Ghanaian Jollof Rice

Serves 5–6

Long-grain rice steams in a rich tomato and pepper sauce seasoned with ginger, garlic, thyme, and curry.

⏱ About 1 hr🍳 Simmer & steam

Ingredients

  • 3 cups long-grain parboiled rice, rinsed
  • 3 large tomatoes, or 1 can (about 400 g) chopped tomatoes
  • 1 red bell pepper and 1 hot pepper, such as Scotch bonnet
  • 1 large onion, 3 garlic cloves, and a thumb-sized piece of ginger
  • 3 tbsp tomato paste and 1/3 cup neutral cooking oil
  • About 2 cups chicken stock, plus a little hot water if needed
  • 1 tsp curry powder, 1 tsp dried thyme, and 1 bay leaf
  • Salt and black pepper to taste

Method

  1. Blend the tomatoes, bell pepper, hot pepper, half the onion, garlic, and ginger until smooth.
  2. Heat the oil in a heavy pot. Chop and cook the remaining onion until softened. Add the tomato paste and fry for 2 minutes.
  3. Pour in the blended mixture. Simmer uncovered for 20–30 minutes, stirring now and then, until reduced and thick.
  4. Stir in the curry powder, thyme, bay leaf, salt, and pepper. Add the rinsed rice and stir to coat it in the sauce.
  5. Pour in enough stock to just cover the rice. Cover tightly and cook over low heat for 25–35 minutes. Check occasionally; if the rice is still firm and the pot is dry, add a small splash of hot water or stock.
  6. When the rice is tender, turn off the heat and let it rest, covered, for 10 minutes. Remove the bay leaf and fluff gently. Serve with fried plantain or chicken.
🍽️07 · Ghana

Fermented corn · cassava

Banku

Serves 4–6

Fermented corn and cassava dough is stirred into a smooth, stretchy staple, traditionally served with okra stew, grilled fish, and pepper sauce.

⏱ About 30 min🍳 Stir & steam

Ingredients

  • 500 g fermented corn dough
  • 500 g fermented cassava dough
  • 600–750 ml water, as needed
  • Salt to taste

Method

  1. Put the corn dough and cassava dough in a bowl. Break up any large lumps and mix them together.
  2. Gradually add water, whisking until you have a smooth, thick batter. Strain it to remove any chaff or remaining lumps.
  3. Pour the mixture into a heavy pot, add salt, and cook over medium heat. Stir continuously as it thickens.
  4. When the mixture becomes stiff, lower the heat. Use a sturdy wooden spoon to press and fold the dough against the sides of the pot until smooth.
  5. If it becomes too stiff before it is cooked, add a small amount of boiling water. Cover and steam over low heat for about 5 minutes.
  6. Stir and fold again. It is ready when smooth, elastic, off-white, and cooked through with no raw taste. Shape into portions and serve hot.
