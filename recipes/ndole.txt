NDOLÉ — CAMEROONIAN BITTERLEAF AND GROUNDNUT STEW

Serves: 6
Preparation: about 40 minutes
Cooking: about 1 hour

INGREDIENTS
- 500 g ndolé (bitterleaf), fresh or thawed
- 300 g raw, shelled peanuts (groundnuts)
- 500 g beef, cut into bite-size pieces
- 1 large onion, chopped
- 3 garlic cloves, minced
- 80 ml red palm oil
- 2 tablespoons ground dried crayfish or dried shrimp (optional but traditional)
- 1 seasoning cube, optional
- Salt and white pepper, to taste
- Water, as needed

METHOD
1. If using fresh bitterleaf, wash it thoroughly. Boil it in water for 5 minutes, drain, and squeeze out the water. Taste a little; if it is too bitter for you, repeat the boiling and squeezing once. Set aside.
2. Put the peanuts in a pot, cover with water, and boil for about 20 minutes until tender. Drain, then blend or pound into a coarse paste with a little water. Avoid making it completely runny.
3. Put the beef in a pot with salt, white pepper, half the onion, and about 250 ml water. Cover and simmer until tender, about 30–45 minutes. Save the stock.
4. Warm the palm oil in a large pot. Add the remaining onion and garlic and cook until soft, about 5 minutes.
5. Stir in the peanut paste and about 250 ml of the reserved beef stock. Cook over medium-low heat for 10 minutes, stirring often so the groundnuts do not stick or burn.
6. Add the cooked beef and ground crayfish. Stir in the bitterleaf and optional seasoning cube. Simmer gently for 15–20 minutes, stirring occasionally, until thick and well combined. Add a little stock if needed.
7. Taste and adjust the salt and pepper. Serve hot with boiled plantain, bobolo, miondo, or rice.

NOTE
Wash and squeeze bitterleaf to adjust its bitterness to your taste. Groundnut paste thickens as it cooks, so stir regularly and add stock gradually.
