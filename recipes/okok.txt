OKOK — CAMEROONIAN GNETUM LEAVES WITH PALM FRUIT AND GROUNDNUT

Serves: 4–6
Preparation: about 30 minutes
Cooking: about 1 hour

INGREDIENTS
- 300 g finely shredded okok leaves (Gnetum africanum)
- 500 g fresh palm fruits, or about 400 ml unsweetened palm-fruit extract
- 200 g roasted peanuts (groundnuts), ground into a thick paste
- 1 teaspoon salt, or to taste
- 1–2 tablespoons sugar, optional (common in some Eton-style versions)
- Water, as needed

METHOD
1. If using fresh palm fruits, rinse them and boil for about 45 minutes, until soft. Drain and pound the fruits in a sturdy bowl or mortar. Add warm water a little at a time, mix, then strain through a sieve to collect a thick palm-fruit extract. If using prepared extract, skip this step.
2. Wash the shredded okok. Steam or boil it in a little water for about 10 minutes, until it begins to soften. Drain. If the pieces are still very coarse, chop them more finely.
3. Put the palm-fruit extract in a pot and bring it to a gentle simmer. Stir in the groundnut paste and cook for about 15–20 minutes, stirring often so it does not catch on the bottom.
4. Add the okok and salt. Simmer gently for another 15–20 minutes, stirring often, until the leaves are tender and the mixture is thick. Add a splash of water if it becomes too thick before the leaves are done.
5. Add sugar if desired, taste, and adjust the seasoning. Serve with boiled cassava, miondo, plantain, or another starchy accompaniment.

NOTE
Okok has regional variations. Some versions are sweet and savory, while others leave out sugar. Palm-fruit extract is different from plain palm oil; use extract or palm-nut cream for the characteristic groundnut-and-palm base.
